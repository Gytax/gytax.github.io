# Baby's first hospitalization
For the first time in my 39 years of life, I've been hospitalized for a few days. I'm writing this to help myself get things off my chest and to provide insight and explanation to what happened to anyone interested.

## What happened?
It was Sunday the 15th of February, I was casually hanging out on a Discord call with a dear friend, watching anime (High school of the dead, it's dumb fun), having fun. 10:30 pm rolled around and I mentioned that I suddenly had a bit of a headache, 10 seconds later it was the worst pain I had ever felt. It felt like somebody grabbed my head with 2 hands and tried to crush it. Another 10 seconds later, I was sweating profusely and immediately turned off all lights.

A slight panic set in, the immediate thought was a migraine attack. My friend had experience with those, so I followed instructions. I transferred the call to my phone and laid in bed. Thinking and hoping it would pass. Spoiler alert; it didn't.

1 agonizing hour and 2 _intense_ vomiting sessions later, I called emergency services. Also a learning moment, the Dutch emergency services cannot be reached from abroad (my friend is from South Africa).

A few short minutes later, emergency services arrived. 2 lovely ladies from the ambulance stumbled into my house, as I had opened the door as instructed and called out for me. All lights were off, I was wearing black clothing, so they couldn't see a thing, naturally.
I greeted them at the door, showed them the light switch and closed my eyes.

After the usual questions of what I was feeling, what I was doing at the time and taking my blood pressure, they suspected a heavy migraine, especially since I've had migraines before. But I've just never had them this bad.
They guided my breathing, injected some painkillers into me through an IV they installed on the spot and called the neurologist, just in case.
After a few words exchanged, I was told they're going to take me with them. So I grabbed my phone, my wallet and my keys and got on the stretcher they'd prepared in the hallway.
While I was being carted away, I realised my friend was still on the phone. She was sobbing heavily, I explained what is happening and promised her that I'd give her an update as soon as I knew more, but right now I was being transported away.

## The hospital
I live very close to the hospital, so carting me down and strapping me in almost took longer than the actual trip to the hospital.
I still had no idea what was going on and mostly kept my eyes closed, because I was _very_ sensitive to light. When being carted in, the few times I opened my eyes, I did see a sign on the wall of the room I was being carted into saying "Acute brain injuries". I distinctly remember that, but I was in survival mode so it didn't fully register.
A very friendly doctor and nurse told me a bunch of stuff in soft voices, about how they were going to do some investigations, while they pumped a bit of Fentanyl and Morfine into me. Needless to say, I don't remember much from the conversation, I just remember they had me squeeze their fingers and put some pressure on a few things and that they'd do a scan.

I was carted to the CT scan room where your head goes into a MASSIVE round machine and they do a brain scan. That was done in 2 minutes and I was carted back to the room I initially arrived in. The doctor told me he was going to call the AMC (Amsterdam Medisch Centrum) to discuss a few things and he'd be back in a few minutes.
Once he was back, he told me they'd do one more scan, a contrast scan this time, to see things a little better, so I was carted back to the CT scan room. I was injected with a fluid that made me feel super warm for like 10 seconds and it felt like I peed my pants, but luckily I didn't.

After the scan and another call and analysis of my CT results, I was told there's good news and bad news. The bad news was that there's a bleeding inside my brain, the good news was that no artery was damaged, so there was no emergency surgery needed, but they would keep me at the hospital at least for the night.

It was a this moment I got injected with a little more painkillers. It was 3am and then I made the call to my parents that you never really want to have to make. "Hi dad, don't panic, but I'm in the hospital." I briefly explained what little I understood of what happened while being completely high on painkillers. Needless to say I screwed up their sleep for the night.
I updated some close friends and work, before I got moved to intensive care, the BCU (brain care unit) to be precise.

On the BCU, I got hooked up with a lot of different things, I don't even know what half of them were for, but they made the machines go beep beep and the doctors seemed happy, so I was happy and I tried to get some sleep...

I asked the doctors what happened to me and apparently I had a [Subarachnoid hemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarachnoid_hemorrhage) (that sounds metal AF). I was told that it can just happen without any good reason and that's what happened to me and surgery for my version (no artery severed) was pointless. I had to wait it out, my body will take care of the excess blood.

I stayed in the hospital from Sunday night until Wednesday around noon. I was a healthy boy, it was just this little brain thing that happened. Halfway through Tuesday I was moved out of the intensive care to regular care and that's when I really started feeling better too. I was positive and thinking I'd be back to normal in a jiffy. The pain was minimal (I was still on painkillers) and I could look at screens again, light didn't affect me that much anymore. I talked to the rehabilitation doctor who asked if I was scared of anything, which didn't really make sense to me. Why would I be scared? I'll just take it a little easy and the rehab doctor told me to _really_ listen to my body. I took that to heart.
I was overjoyed when I was told I could go home on Wednesday. My parents were already on their way, so I called them to come pick me up because I could go home!

## So all good?
When travelling downstairs from the hospital, I was super excited, but when walking over to the counter to get the take-home meds, I noticed I got a little dizzy. This was the first inkling that perhaps I wasn't as fit as I thought.
My parents drove me home and we quickly stopped at the grocery store along the way, I wanted to join. I was asked if I was sure I wanted to join, but I was adamant....
It was then that I realized; I'm not okay. Every little thing that required brain power was 10x harder than usual. When I walked through my door into my apartment (which was half remodeled, bless my parents, they took _care_ of my apartment during the days they stayed here), I broke down. Emotions were flowing, I was going through it and I started to realise what had actually happened.

## What now?
My brain had an oopsie and while there's no lasting damage, it is having a very very hard time recovering. Which results in me being emotional, weak, not able to exert myself, not able use my brain for longer amounts of time.
I have a persistent headache, something I will likely have for a few weeks. I have meds for that, but those are the super heavy kind and I'll be out of them this weekend, so paracetamol will have to do from then on. The cause of the headache has been described to me to think of it like a bruise. When you hit your arm, you get a bruise, a little bulge that turns purple. That is what's happening in my brain, but there's not really any room for bulges in my skull, it's all to TOIGHT. That's why it hurts so much.

I have the best friends, lovely colleagues, amazing parents and a good support system around me. I will require help with a lot of things, I haven't left the house since I got home and I don't want to. I understand now why the rehabilitation doctor asked if I was scared. _Everything_ is heavy and _everything_ is potentially another source of a bad headache.

I am taking babysteps, asking friends to do groceries for me (even though it makes me super uncomfortable not being able to do things myself), really listening to my body. When I have an inkling of stress, I stop what I'm doing and give my body rest.

I'm trying to get used to the pain that I wake up with, so far the results are middling. I video call my parents multiple times per day to check in and see how things are going, I'm in good contact with my friends.

For now, I have no idea how long I will need. My next appointment with the rehabilitation doctor is on the 16th of March, until that time I'll be recovering. Setting small goals for myself and taking baby-steps.

If you're reading this, you likely know me. Let me use this to say thank you for the kind words you've all given me, the cards you've sent, the re-assuring messages and your willingness to help me with whatever I need. It really means the world!

One more special shoutout to Lola, who I was on call with when it all happened. If it wasn't for you, Lola, I'm not sure I would have called emergency services and I don't know in what state I would have woken up. I'm eternally grateful to you for that <3

~ Kevin
